## Facebook description
Everyone climbs Sigiriya, but few hear its secrets. Around 477 CE Prince Kashyapa seized his father's throne. The chronicle says he walled his father up alive, and his brother fled to India to raise an army. So Kashyapa built his capital on a granite rock about 180 metres high.

The final stairs once led between the paws and into the mouth of a giant brick lion. Only the paws survive, dug out by H.C.P. Bell in 1898. The cliff was a gallery: the old verses speak of 500 painted women, and about 20 remain. Over a thousand years ago, visitors scratched poems to them on the polished Mirror Wall. Senarat Paranavitana read 685 of them in 1956, and about 800 more have been read since. Down in the water gardens, fountains fed by gravity from a slightly higher moat still rise in the rainy season.

Kashyapa's end came in 495 CE: his elephant turned from a swamp, his army thought he was fleeing, and he took his own life. The rock became a Buddhist monastery. Some, like former Archaeology Commissioner Dr Raja de Silva, argue it was a monastery all along.

- **Style:** Watercolor Brush, as chosen. No palette was supplied, so the colours come from the style and the subject: warm paper, rock ochre and burnt sienna, jungle greens, water blue, dusk violet for the ending, and **vermilion kept only for the four numbered "secret" seals** (and the king's parasol, the brother's route and the 800 figure).

- **Cautious wording:** the walling-up and the elephant come from the chronicle (Cūḷavaṃsa), so the film says "the chronicle says". Fresco counts differ between sources (18–22), so the film says "about 20". The monastery idea is a minority view, so it's asked as a question and credited to Raja de Silva. - **Transitions:** every scene change is a 0.8 s eased dissolve. The style's own wipes, mist transitions and page turns are not used between scenes; mist only appears inside a scene (the lion fading, the battle ending).
- **Frame:** the art fills the frame edge to edge at every camera position. Titles, numbers and notes sit inside x 162–918, y 288–1536. Captions are one line at y≈1600, light text with a dark halo.
- **People:** simple drawn figures only (painted women, soldiers, monks, a rider), no real people.
- **Voice:** bm_george (British male, Kokoro). Warm and unhurried; it suits a history-and-legend story and scored best on the speech check.
- **Music:** an original score in D (harp, marimba, flute, glockenspiel, strings, cellos, pizzicato double bass) from CC0 samples.
