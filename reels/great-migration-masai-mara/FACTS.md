# FACTS — The Great Migration (Serengeti–Masai Mara)

Checked 24 Nov 2026. Each on-screen or spoken claim, its source and date. Estimates are labelled.

| # | Claim used in the film | Source (date) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The herds move in a clockwise loop between Tanzania's Serengeti and Kenya's Masai Mara, following seasonal rain | National Geographic, "Why the wildebeest is the unlikely king of the Serengeti" (30 Nov 2021); Nat Geo, "How wildebeest reshape the Serengeti…" |
| 2 | Round trip of about 800 km (≈500 miles) | National Geographic, "How wildebeest reshape the Serengeti during Africa's Great Migration" |
| 3 | Wildebeest are joined by zebra and gazelle | Univ. of Glasgow news (Aug 2014); National Geographic (as above) |
| 4 | Movement tracks fresh grass: models best explained by intake rate and nitrogen (protein) of green grass | Holdo, Holt & Fryxell, *American Naturalist* 2009 (PubMed 19243258) |
| 5 | Southern Serengeti grass is richer in phosphorus (a mineral for bones and milk) than Mara grass | Open University OpenLearn, "Studying mammals: plant predators", §9 |
| 6 | Calving on the southern short-grass plains, Jan–Mar, peak in February; up to ~8,000 calves born a day (estimate) | NASA Earth Observatory, "Circling the Serengeti"; Nat Geo (as above); Discover Wildlife |
| 7 | Calves stand within minutes of birth | Discover Africa, calving season (field guide summary); People Daily (Apr 2020) |
| 8 | The Mara River is the only (reliable) water source in the dry season; herds in the Mara roughly July–October | Univ. of Glasgow news (Aug 2014); BBC Wildlife "Wildebeest and water on the wane" (30 Jul 2020) |
| 9 | Mass drownings happen nearly every year; average ≈6,250 wildebeest drown a year (estimate) | Subalusky et al., *PNAS* 114:7647 (2017); National Geographic (19 Jun 2017) |
| 10 | Crocodiles eat only about 2% of carcasses | Axios on Subalusky et al. (15 Dec 2017) |
| 11 | Bones take about 7 years to decompose, feeding the river long-term | Subalusky et al., *PNAS* 2017 (PMC5530641 abstract) |
| 12 | Herd long estimated at ~1.3 million from aerial surveys, largely unchanged since the 1970s | University of Oxford news (Sep 2025) |
| 13 | AI count of satellite images (Aug 2022 & 2023) found fewer than 600,000 (≈503,000–533,000 in 2023); may reflect shifted routes, not necessarily collapse | University of Oxford news on *PNAS Nexus* paper (9 Sep 2025) |
| 14 | Pressures: farms, fencing and infrastructure fragmenting habitat; climate change | University of Oxford news (Sep 2025) |
| 15 | The Mara River rises in Kenya's Mau Forest; deforestation there and water abstraction threaten its flow | Kenya News Agency / LVBC (22 Jul 2026); The Star Kenya (11 Sep 2025); Nation Africa |

Dropped (could not verify consistently): "2 million animals", precise zebra/gazelle totals (sources range 200,000–300,000), "200,000 die each cycle".
