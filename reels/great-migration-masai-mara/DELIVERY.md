## Facebook description
Every year, wildebeest, zebra and gazelle follow the rain in a clockwise loop of about 800 km between Tanzania's Serengeti and Kenya's Masai Mara. They are chasing fresh grass, rich in protein and minerals. Early in the year, on the southern short-grass plains, up to 8,000 calves can be born in a day – and they stand within minutes.

From around July the herds reach the Mara River, the only reliable dry-season water. Crossing it is the most dangerous moment of the year: researchers estimate around 6,250 drown in an average year. But the river gains – the bones feed it for about seven years.

For decades the herd was put at 1.3 million. In 2025 an AI count of satellite images found fewer than 600,000 – possibly a shifted route, not a collapse, but fences, farms and a shrinking river all press on the path.

Sources: Subalusky et al., PNAS (2017); University of Oxford / PNAS Nexus (Sep 2025); NASA Earth Observatory; National Geographic; Holdo et al., American Naturalist (2009); Kenya News Agency / LVBC (Jul 2026).
