## Facebook description
Meet thambili, Sri Lanka's king coconut. Its bright orange husk sets it apart, and in Sri Lanka a guest is traditionally welcomed with a glass of its water. In 2025 Sri Lanka exported nearly $9 million worth, about 95% of it to Dubai, and demand now outpaces supply. Researchers are working towards protected (GI) status for the name.

So what is it? Sri Lanka's coconuts fall into three groups: tall, dwarf and king. The king coconut is a semi-tall palm. Its water is about 95% water, with 5–6 g of natural sugar per 100 ml (mostly glucose and fructose), and potassium makes up more than half of its minerals.

How does it travel? Nuts are picked 7–8 months after pollination, from the bunch at leaf 14, counting the youngest leaf as 1. Within 24–36 hours they're cleaned, wrapped in film, netted and packed 6–8 to a box. Kept at 13–15 °C they stay fresh for about four weeks; in tropical heat, just four days.

Sources: Coconut Research Institute of Sri Lanka, Technical guidelines to enhance shelf life of tender king coconut for export; Ediriweera, CORD (1996); Gunathilake & Rathnayake, CORD (2012); Ekanayake et al., COCOS (2010); Shayanthavi et al., Food Chemistry Advances (2024); The Morning (13 Oct 2026; 1 Jul 2026); Ada Derana (Jun 2026).
