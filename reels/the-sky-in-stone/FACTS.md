# FACTS — "The sky in stone" (Everyday Frequency reel)
Checked 26 Nov 2026. Every number and claim in the film, with source and date. Estimates are labelled.

| # | Claim in film | Source (date) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Newgrange (Ireland) was built c. 3200 BC, "over five thousand years ago"; older than Stonehenge and the Giza pyramids | newgrange.com (World Heritage Site page, accessed Nov 2026): "Constructed around 5,200 years ago (3,200 B.C.)"; Meath County Council heritage page (built c. 3200 BC); World History Encyclopedia (Newgrange) | date is an archaeological estimate (radiocarbon) |
| 2 | Around the winter solstice, sunlight enters through a slot ("roof-box") above the door and lights the chamber floor | Irish Times, "Shedding light on winter solstice at Newgrange" (2014): beam 19–23 Dec, lasts 17 minutes; Ray, *Nature* 337:343 (1989): light reaches chamber floor ~4.5 min after sunrise | some archaeologists argue the roof-box was reconstructed in the 1960s–70s (Irish Times, 2016); the passage's solstice orientation is not disputed. Film says "a slot above the door", not that the box is untouched |
| 3 | Passage about 19 m long; on-screen "≈19 M" | Irish Times, "1,000 years older than Stonehenge": "19-metre-long" passage | other sources give 17–18 m; labelled approximate |
| 4 | The light show lasts about 17 minutes (on-screen "≈17 MIN") | Irish Times (2014); Wikipedia "Roofbox" | weather permitting |
| 5 | Great Pyramid of Giza aligned to the cardinal points to better than 4 arcminutes = one-fifteenth of a degree | Glen Dash, *Journal of Ancient Egyptian Architecture* (2017), reported by Live Science / Smithsonian (Feb 2018) | "about 0.067° counter-clockwise" |
| 6 | Great Pyramid built about 4,500 years ago (on-screen "c. 2560 BC") | Live Science (2018): "About 4,500 years ago … Khufu" | conventional date c. 2560 BC (estimate) |
| 7 | Equinox method: a rod's shadow tip on the equinox runs almost exactly east–west; may be how the pyramid was aligned | Dash (2017/2018), Live Science; Smithsonian (2018) | a proposed method; star methods are also proposed. Film says "by watching", not that this is proven |
| 8 | El Castillo (Temple of Kukulcán), Chichén Itzá, Mexico: four stairways of 91 steps; with the top platform = 365, the days of the year | Britannica, "What's inside the pyramid at Chichén Itzá?"; National Geographic; Exploratorium | south stair is eroded today (Wikipedia) |
| 9 | At the equinoxes a shadow "serpent" appears on the north stairway | National Geographic; Exploratorium ("viewable for a week before and after each equinox") | whether it was designed is debated; on screen only as "EQUINOX SHADOW" |
| 10 | Angkor Wat, Cambodia (12th century, Suryavarman II, c. 1113–1150): five central towers = peaks of Mount Meru, home of the gods; moat = cosmic ocean | NASA Earth Observatory (Angkor Wat); Univ. of Pittsburgh EAGLE (Angkor Wat); World History Encyclopedia (2020) | |
| 11 | Angkor Wat faces west; sun rises over the central tower at the spring equinox | World History Encyclopedia; Mental Floss (Angkor Wat facts) | equinox sunrise shown as a visual only |
| 12 | For early farming societies the solar year (solstices, equinoxes) marked seasons for planting, harvest and ritual | Exploratorium, Ancient Observatories ("the Maya could easily have tracked the seasons"); Irish Times (Newgrange, renewal at turn of the year) | general framing, not a statistic |
