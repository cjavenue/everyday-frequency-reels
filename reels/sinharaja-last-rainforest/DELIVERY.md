## Facebook description
Sinharaja, in Sri Lanka's wet south-west, is the country's last surviving primary rainforest: a strip of forested hills about 21 km long, a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve since 1978 and a World Heritage Site since 1988. More than 60% of its trees are endemic to Sri Lanka, 15 of the island's 20 endemic mammals live here, and its "bird waves" – mixed flocks of around 40 birds from about 11 species – have been studied since 1981.

It nearly vanished. In the 1970s about 1,400 hectares were selectively logged to feed a plywood mill, until public protest stopped it in 1977. Today the IUCN rates its outlook as "significant concern", with tea plantations at the edges, roads, illegal gem mining, hotels, invasive plants and a changing climate all adding pressure.

Its streams feed the Kalu and Gin rivers and its trees store carbon. Protecting it means keeping roads out, guarding the edges and growing forest corridors to link it with other forests. A primary forest can't be replanted – it can only be protected.
