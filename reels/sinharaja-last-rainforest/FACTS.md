# FACTS — Sinharaja: Sri Lanka's last rainforest

Checked 24 Nov 2026. Every number on screen or in the voice is listed here. Figures from the newest authoritative source are used; where sources disagree, the conflict is noted and the safer figure or wording is used.

| # | Claim in the film | Source (date) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sinharaja is Sri Lanka's last viable area of primary tropical rainforest | UNESCO World Heritage Centre, list entry 405 (whc.unesco.org/en/list/405) | Film says "last surviving primary rainforest" |
| 2 | In the wet south-west of Sri Lanka | UNESCO 405; UNEP-WCMC datasheet (1986, updated May 2011) | |
| 3 | About 21 km long | UNEP-WCMC datasheet ("21 km by 4 km strip"); Wikipedia gives max 7 km north–south | Only the length is used |
| 4 | UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1988; Biosphere Reserve since 1978 | UNESCO 405; UNEP-WCMC datasheet | WH area 8,864 ha |
| 5 | More than 60% of its trees are endemic (found nowhere else) | UNESCO 405 ("More than 60% of the trees are endemic"); IUCN World Heritage Outlook 2025 | "Endemic" = endemic to Sri Lanka. Voice says "found nowhere else on Earth" — true of Sri Lankan endemics outside Sri Lanka; screen note reads "endemic to Sri Lanka" |
| 6 | 15 of Sri Lanka's 20 endemic mammals live here (75%) | IUCN World Heritage Outlook 2025 (finalised 11 Oct 2025), citing Bambaradeniya et al. 2003 | |
| 7 | Purple-faced langur occurs in the reserve | UNESCO 405 (threatened species list: "endemic purple-faced langur") | Drawn as a simple painted figure |
| 8 | In the eastern corner, 10 shrub-frog (*Pseudophilautus*) species, 5 Critically Endangered | IUCN World Heritage Outlook 2025 | Voice: "five of its shrub frogs are critically endangered" with "in one corner alone" |
| 9 | Mixed-species bird flocks ("bird waves") average ~41 birds and ~11 species | Kotagama & Goodale, *Forktail* 20 (2004): 10.9 species, 41.3 individuals per flock | Voice: "around forty birds from about eleven species" |
| 10 | Flock study running since 1981 | Kotagama & Goodale 2004 (fieldwork 1981–84, 1995–98); Jetwing Eco / de Silva Wijeyeratne (2007): begun 1981 | Screen: "studied since 1981" |
| 11 | Timber extraction from 1968; about 1,400 ha selectively logged in the 1970s for plywood | UNEP-WCMC datasheet; plywood mill at Kosgama: Daily Mirror editorial; NSF Sri Lanka paper | Screen: "~1,400 ha logged" (estimate) |
| 12 | Public protest; logging stopped in 1977 | UNEP-WCMC datasheet ("logging was banned in 1977"); Sanctuary Nature Foundation (Lockwood) | |
| 13 | IUCN 2025 Conservation Outlook: "Significant concern" | IUCN World Heritage Outlook, site 1022, finalised 11 Oct 2025 | |
| 14 | High threats named: tourism (hotels), illegal logging and collection, forest fires, illegal gem mining, invasive species, roads, climate change; encroachment by tea plantations | IUCN World Heritage Outlook 2025 | |
| 15 | Road widening Neluwa–Deniyaya: ~1,320 m inside the reserve; legal challenge allowed by Court of Appeal | The Morning, 22 Jun 2025 | Background only, not in the voice |
| 16 | Streams drain to the Kalu Ganga (north) and Gin Ganga (south) | UNEP-WCMC datasheet; UNESCO; Lakpura | |
| 17 | Carbon sequestration is one of its benefits | IUCN World Heritage Outlook 2025 (listed benefit, no quantity) | No number used |
| 18 | Corridors: plantation companies agreed to release some tea land to link Sinharaja with Walankanda–Delwala forests | IUCN World Heritage Outlook 2025 | Voice: "growing forest corridors to link it to other forests" |

## Dropped (couldn't verify or sources conflict)
- Number of endemic birds (sources give 19, 20, 21 or 33; 85% or 95%): left out.
- "36,000 ha" area (Wikipedia): conflicts with UNESCO's 8,864 ha; left out.
- Rainfall figures (2,500 mm+ vs 3,600–5,000 mm in secondary sites): left out.
- Hotel-inside-the-reserve claims: the Forest Department says the hotel in question is 5 km away (Ada Derana); film says only "hotels" as an IUCN-rated pressure.
