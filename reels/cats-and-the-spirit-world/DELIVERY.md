## Facebook description
Ever caught your cat staring at an empty corner? For thousands of years people have linked cats with the spirit world. In ancient Egypt cats were seen as living images of Bastet, a goddess who guarded the home. Millions were mummified as offerings, and Herodotus wrote that a family would shave off their eyebrows when their cat died.

In medieval Europe the mood turned dark. A papal letter of 1233 described a black cat at a devil-worship rite, and black cats became tied to witches. Yet in Britain and Japan a black cat crossing your path is lucky. Japanese folklore also says a very old cat can become a bakeneko, a shape-shifting spirit.

The real answer is their senses. Cats hear up to about 64,000 Hz, while we stop near 20,000. Mice squeak in ultrasound, and cats can see with about a sixth of the light we need. The "ghost" is usually a sound or a flicker that only the cat can catch.

Sources: Herodotus, Histories 2.66; Smithsonian Magazine (2025); University of Geneva, Visual Contagions; Snopes (2023); Cats Protection; IFAW; Scientific Reports 10:2637 (2020); University of Illinois (ANSC 207).
