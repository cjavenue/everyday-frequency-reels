# FACTS — Cats and the spirit world

Checked 26 November 2026. Every claim in the film, with its source.

| # | Claim in the film | Source (accessed 26 Nov 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In ancient Egypt the domestic cat was seen as a living embodiment of Bastet, a goddess who protected the household. | Wikipedia, "Cats in ancient Egypt" (citing Malek, *The Cat in Ancient Egypt*); Smithsonian Magazine (2025): cats "revered as earthly embodiments of the goddess Bastet". |
| 2 | Millions of cats were mummified as offerings to the gods. | Smithsonian Magazine, "How Did Ancient Egypt's Sacred Cats Live?" (2025, on a Beni Hassan skull study): "millions of cats were once mummified … to honor the gods"; at least 20 animal cemeteries. Wikipedia: votive mummies for festivals and pilgrims, Late Period from 664 BC. |
| 3 | Herodotus wrote that when a household cat died (of natural causes), everyone in the house shaved off their eyebrows. | Herodotus, *Histories* 2.66 (Godley translation, Perseus): "The occupants of a house where a cat has died a natural death shave their eyebrows". Written c. 430 BC (estimate; the *Histories* date to c. 430–425 BC). |
| 4 | A papal letter of 1233 (Vox in Rama, Pope Gregory IX, 13 June 1233) describes a black cat appearing at a devil-worship rite; black cats became linked with witches in European folklore. | University of Geneva, Visual Contagions, "The black cat": the bull "describes the appearance of a large black cat atop a statue, an avatar of the devil". Snopes (8 Nov 2023) confirms the letter is real (and that the "cat massacre" story is a late-1990s myth, so the film does not claim it). History.com on cats and witches. |
| 5 | In Britain and Japan a black cat crossing your path is considered lucky; British and Irish sailors kept black cats aboard for luck. | Cats Protection (UK), "Black cat superstitions"; International Cat Care; Wikipedia "Black cat" (positive omen in Britain and Ireland); Kinship UK. |
| 6 | In Japanese folklore an old cat can become a bakeneko, a supernatural cat often said to shape-shift. | Wikipedia "Bakeneko" (folk beliefs across Japan about aged cats turning into bakeneko); University of Oregon GLAM, Yōkai sugoroku notes (bakeneko "imagined as including shape-shifting"). |
| 7 | Cats hear up to about 64,000 Hz; humans up to about 20,000 Hz. | IFAW, "Animals with the best hearing" (cats 45–64,000 Hz; humans ~20 Hz–20 kHz); PangoVet (vet-reviewed). |
| 8 | Mice call in ultrasound, above the range of human hearing. | Scientific Reports 10:2637 (2020): adult mouse ultrasonic vocalisations span 30–110 kHz, "signals above the range of human hearing"; AMNH. |
| 9 | Cats can see using about one-sixth of the light humans need; a reflective layer behind the retina (tapetum lucidum) gives light a second pass and makes their eyes shine. | University of Illinois ANSC 207 course notes ("approximately 1/6th the amount of the light"); Wikipedia "Cat senses"; GBH/The Conversation (2022). Cats still cannot see in total darkness. |
| 10 | Conclusion: a cat "staring at nothing" is usually reacting to sounds, movement or light we can't sense. | Inference from 7–9, presented as "usually", not as proven fact. |

## Dropped (couldn't verify well enough)
- "Killing a cat was punishable by death in Egypt" — reported by ancient writers (Diodorus) but the law's extent is unclear.
- "Vox in Rama led to mass cat killings that caused the Black Death" — debunked (Snopes, 2023).
- Ultraviolet vision in cats — evidence is about lens transmission, not proven perception.
